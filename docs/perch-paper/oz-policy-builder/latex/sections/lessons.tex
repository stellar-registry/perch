We propose six changes; none is built.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Recording as authoring.} A front end that, like oz-policy-builder's
synthesiser, starts from a recorded transaction and emits a Perch grant document
for the owner to review. A recording shows calls, not intent, so the owner would
still choose the cap, its window and the expiry.
\item \textbf{Simulating before install.} A wallet that installs a grant only
after running the intended calls through the deployed account's own evaluator in
enforcing simulation, as oz-policy-builder verifies a predicate with the deployed
interpreter's \code{check}.
\item \textbf{Minimality checks.} A check that flags a rule wider than the
recorded calls need, as oz-policy-builder drops each conjunct in turn, and an
overlap scan that refuses a capped rule a key it names could bypass through
another rule, since a Perch key holds the union of every rule naming it.
\item \textbf{Keeping a record across a limit change.} Keeping a rule's
spending record when only its limit changes, as OpenZeppelin's
\code{spending\_limit} does.
\item \textbf{A corpus of real transactions.} Real Soroban transactions run
through Perch's compiler, lowering and evaluator, beside the
\fact{lean-doc-traces} Lean-generated traces its integration suite runs today.
\item \textbf{An audit before claiming maturity.} An external audit of the
Perch contracts (account, compiler, recovery controller, verifiers,
zero-knowledge pool and adapter, registry) and of rule-core's evaluator. Until
then the authors recommend that Perch not hold real funds.
\end{enumerate}
