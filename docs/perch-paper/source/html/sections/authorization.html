If the user refuses, nothing is signed or sent. If the user signs, the wallet submits the transaction and the account decides in a fixed order. Guards first refuse requests the account never allows through a grant, such as a call to its adopted recovery contracts. The account looks up each named rule and its spending record. Its decision function, rule_eval::check_auth in the rule-core library {{cite:rule-core}}, checks every call against its rule and computes the new spending records. It reads no storage and checks no signature, and it is the part the proofs cover. Only if it accepts does a verifier contract check the app's signature, and only if that passes does the account store the new records. A refusal at any step writes nothing. The decision function reports a refusal as contract error {{fact:auth-error-rule-eval-refused}}, or {{fact:auth-error-stale-scope}} for a grant from before a recovery.

The app signs as an external signer, a key the account checks through a verifier, such as an Ed25519 key or a passkey. Its signature binds the account and the rules the transaction names, so a relayer cannot move the payment to another rule the key belongs to, such as an uncapped one. A delegated signer, another Stellar address that approves through the host, does not bind the rule choice in Perch; OpenZeppelin {{fact:oz-v080-tag}} binds it for both kinds.

A stolen app key meets the same checks

Whoever holds the app's key, the app itself or a thief, goes through the same decision. Under the shop grant that key can send at most {{fact:shop-cap-xlm}} XLM per {{fact:shop-period-ledgers}} ledgers to the shop and nothing else: no other token, recipient or function, and no call to the account. Any grant it narrows for a helper allows nothing its own grant does not. The owner ends a grant with one call, remove_grant, which deletes its rules and spending records, and a grant's rules also stop at their expiry. If the owner recovers the account after losing a key, one counter on the account advances, and every earlier grant, with every grant narrowed from one, stops authorizing in that same transaction. The vision paper covers the owner's key, guardians and the verifier registry.
