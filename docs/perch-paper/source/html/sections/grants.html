Window boundaries, replacement, and what the model leaves out

The rule counts accepted positive charges in the trailing ledger window. This example is a model, not a transaction or fee estimate. The window theorem starts from empty records for one unchanged cap; negative charges and positive charges at backwards ledgers are rejected. Replacing a rule or changing its cap can reset its record; the cap is not a lifetime limit across owner reconfiguration.

For the same cap and period, a sliding window avoids spending a full fixed-period allowance on both sides of a reset. Some systems deliberately use fixed periods; MetaMask's ERC20PeriodTransferEnforcer is one example. {{cite:metamask-delegation}} The verification companion gives the invariant and hypotheses.
