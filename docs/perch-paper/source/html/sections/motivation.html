Perch lets an account give an app or agent authority whose limits the account itself enforces.

On Stellar, a smart account is a contract that holds a user's funds and approves the calls made in its name; Soroban is the platform that runs such contracts. An app that spends for a user today has two options. It can ask the user to approve every call, which does not work for an agent that pays a service many times an hour. Or it can hold a key that can do anything the account can, so that whoever steals the app's key can empty the account. Perch adds a third. The owner installs a grant, a written permission with limits, and the account checks every call the app makes in its name against it. Perch accounts run on Stellar through the {{fact:wallet-name}} wallet. {{ref:fig:fig-overview}} shows who does what.

This paper follows one request from start to finish:

  1. An app asks the user's Perch account to pay for an order.
  2. The sign dialog says in plain words what the request asks for, with the full tree of authority under Details.
  3. It previews the request's effects in the simulator.
  4. The user signs or refuses, and the account decides.
  5. The same request runs against an OpenZeppelin account.

An app asks the account to pay for an order

Every request starts from a grant the owner installed. The paper's running example is the shop grant ({{ref:fig:fig-shop-grant}}). It lets an app send XLM, Stellar's native currency, from the account to one shop and no one else, at most {{fact:shop-cap-xlm}} XLM in any {{fact:shop-period-ledgers}} ledgers, {{fact:shop-period-minutes}} at about {{fact:nominal-ledger-seconds}} seconds a ledger. The grant has one rule, which names the contract (the XLM token), the function (transfer), the recipient, the key that must sign (the app's) and a cap; a rule may also stop at an expiry ledger. The owner installs a grant document like this one with one call to the account, apply_grant, after a document compiler contract validates it. Wallets can build documents with the npm package {{fact:perch-js-package}}.

{{fig:fig-shop-grant}}

The sign dialog in step 4 uses a similar grant from a toy café app: one rule that lets the café's key send XLM, capped at 10 XLM per {{fact:shop-period-ledgers}} ledgers, with 3 XLM already spent. Unlike the shop grant, it does not pin the recipient. The café's checkout asks the account to pay for an order. The asker may also be a helper, such as a sub-agent, holding a narrower grant. Attenuation derives such a grant from the app's with rule-core's {{fact:attenuation-steps}} narrowing steps, among them dropping a rule, removing functions, adding an argument condition, raising a signer threshold, lowering a cap, lengthening its period and moving the expiry earlier. From the shop grant, for example, a helper can receive a grant that pays the same shop at most 2 XLM per {{fact:shop-period-ledgers}} ledgers.
