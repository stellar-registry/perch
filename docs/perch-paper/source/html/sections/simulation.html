What this comparison does—and does not—show

The browser runs the pinned contracts in soroban-sim, offline, with enforcing authorization. It is not an exact cost benchmark. Perch uses an external Ed25519 signer; this oz-policy-builder interpreter requires a delegated signer. These are disclosed configuration differences.

Both sides accept 6 XLM and refuse a further 5. The demo can then remove OpenZeppelin's spending policy with owner authority; subsequent payments differ because the configurations now differ. That is a composition experiment, not an attacker bypass. Re-adding that policy resets its history, just as replacing or changing a Perch cap can reset its record.
