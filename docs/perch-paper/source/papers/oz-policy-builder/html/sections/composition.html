oz-policy-builder gets its guarantees by composition: each contract checks one thing, and the account runs them together. That is a deliberate trade, and it buys flexibility.

What oz-policy-builder documents

  • Rules judge one call. "A Stellar call that authorises two contexts (a Blend submit and its token transfer) needs a rule for each, because the interpreter sees one authorised call at a time" (docs/architecture.md:307-309). The remedy for relating calls is the execution adapter: a rule bound to the adapter is honoured only for a call in a batch the adapter checked, and a predicate on the adapter's execute can relate the calls of that batch (docs/stellar.md:191-195).
  • Totals come from another policy. The interpreter "does not see what a transaction moved, keep rolling totals or count calls. Amount limits over time come from the allowance and its expiry, or from OpenZeppelin's spending_limit beside the interpreter" (docs/stellar.md:404-407). The synthesiser attaches spending_limit only on transfer, because that policy refuses every other call (packages/policy-synth/src/install.ts:957-967). Its window has the same boundary as Perch's, (C − P, C], with at most {{fact:oz-spending-history-max}} entries (oz8:policies/spending_limit.rs:9-12, 169). It is outside oz-policy-builder's audit scope (docs/audit-overview.md:76-85), but OpenZeppelin publishes audit reports for its library, and its {{fact:oz-v080-tag}} diff audit lists policies/spending_limit.rs and smart_account/storage.rs in scope (oz8:audits/, v0.8.0 report, p. 4).
  • Several signers need a threshold policy. "An install with several signers needs a threshold (OpenZeppelin simple_threshold or weighted_threshold) that one signer cannot meet alone. Without one, any single signer could act alone" (README.md:122-125). The tool refuses such an install unless the owners set anyOne.
  • Changing and ending a rule are separate steps. The interpreter stores a hash of a policed rule's signer set and refuses with error {{fact:opb-error-signers-changed}} once the set changes, so changing who signs means adding a new rule and removing the old one, or removing the policy and adding it again with the policy admins' authorization (docs/stellar.md:552-554). Rules are removed one at a time. Removing a rule with only rule 0's signatures leaves the interpreter's entry for it until the entry archives, because the interpreter's uninstall needs the policy admins' own authorization; the account gives every new rule a fresh ID and never reads that entry again (docs/stellar.md:548-562).

What composition buys

The README states the aim: "one audit-once engine, and every policy is declarative data fed into it, so a new combination of constraints needs no new contract and no re-audit" (README.md:21-24). The interpreter has no admin and no upgrade path (docs/architecture.md:153).

Composition also lets a rule consult the world. A predicate can read another contract during enforce, for example an oracle price or a registry's list, up to the instance's max_reads, {{fact:opb-interpreter-max-reads}} on the testnet pin (docs/stellar.md:369-394). oz-policy-builder documents the care this needs: a read sees state after anything the submitter ran earlier in the same transaction, so a rule should read only values the submitter cannot move and restore. And because the decision is assembled from OpenZeppelin policies, OpenZeppelin's threshold policies and spending_limit, which other OpenZeppelin accounts also run, sit beside the interpreter.

What Perch does instead, and what it costs

A Perch grant is one document, lowered into rules that the account stores with their spending records and decides with one function: target, functions, argument conditions, threshold, expiry and window cap together, before any signer is authenticated.

The costs are real. The rule language is small: {{fact:doc-conditions-count}} conjunctive argument conditions, no or and no not, no reads of other contracts, and no bound on a single call's amount. A condition the language lacks needs new evaluator code, new proofs and a new account build, which a deployed account reaches only through its own upgrade, scheduled by the owner and delayed {{fact:upgrade-delay-ledgers}} ledgers ({{fact:upgrade-delay-days}}). Nothing can be swapped in: an account cannot attach a policy someone else wrote and audited, and OpenZeppelin's add-ons do not apply to grant rules. And the one place that decides must be right, which is why the proofs and their limits matter here ({{ref:sec:perch-stronger}}).
