Recovery invalidation is proved in an abstract account with two events. The model has no Rust counterpart; the account's code is tested against it.

The account model ({{ref:def:account-events}}) has a state of three parts, the account's address, its account document and its grant generation, and two events: apply a document, which keeps the generation, and complete a recovery, which installs the recovery target and advances the generation by one. A grant is issued in a state by lowering it under that state's address, excluded set and generation. Everything else the account does, storage, signatures, the credential index, the recovery controller's checks, is outside the model.

{{statements:T10}}

{{ref:stmt:recovery-invalidation}} has two parts. In the account model, the generation counts completed recoveries exactly, and a grant issued before one, or derived from such a grant, accepts no request with a call in it at the new generation. In the translation, the evaluator refuses a rule whose generation is not the one passed in (authorize_generation), which is the half that runs on chain. Administrative rules carry no generation and are unaffected.

What joins the two is obligation O3: the account passes its stored generation to the evaluator, advances it only on a completed recovery, and refuses on overflow. That is tested in Perch's integration suite, executed in the earlier live experiment and recorded on testnet (the Perch vision), and rule-core lists it as unproven against Perch ({{ref:tab:tab-obligations}}). The instance in the card shows two grants at generation 0 refusing every nonempty batch after one completion.
