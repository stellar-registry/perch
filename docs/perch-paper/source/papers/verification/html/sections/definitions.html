Nine definitions fix what the theorems mean: contexts, rules, the decision, windows, derivation, documents, account events, the host, and denotation.

Notation. Lists carry an arrow: \(\vec c = c_1, \dots, c_n\) is the contexts of one authorization. \(r_i = R_{j_i}\) is the rule chosen for context \(c_i\). A rule's last ledger \(u_r\) is \(\infty\), and its generation \(g_r\) is \(\bot\), when absent. \(\varnothing\) is the empty records, one empty record per rule. \(|\cdot|\) is length or cardinality. Ledgers are natural numbers. Definitions 1 to 5 also appear in the Perch paper; Definitions 6 to 9 are needed only here.

Ending authority. Four words recur in the theorems about recovery. A grant is invalidated when the account's generation passes the generation it was stamped with; it stays stored and refuses every nonempty batch. Removal (remove_scope, by the owner) deletes a grant's rules, tags and spending records. Collection (gc_scope, by anyone) deletes the storage of an invalidated grant and keeps the credential index. Archival is a lapse of an entry's time to live: the entry is unavailable until restored, not absent.
