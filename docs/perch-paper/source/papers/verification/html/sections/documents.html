A grant document becomes stored rules through lowering. Two theorems cover that step: what lowering refuses, and what the result allows once attenuated and accepted.

The account installs a grant by calling the document compiler, which validates the JSON and returns a model of the document, and then lowering that model with rule-core's lower_scoped under its own address \(A\), its excluded set \(X\) and its current generation \(g\) ({{ref:def:documents-and-lowering}}). In the account, \(X\) holds its adopted recovery controller and the zero-knowledge adapter and pool of its recovery configuration; lowering refuses any rule whose target is \(A\) or in \(X\).

Three hypotheses

  • Doc.Valid: every rule that requires all its signers names distinct signers, fewer than \(2^{32}\) of them, and no rule has a not-after ledger of 0. That Perch's compiler accepts only valid documents is recorded as unproven ({{fact:claim-perch-validation}}; obligation O5).
  • LowerRoom: the document's lists fit the evaluator's bounded vectors, so the translated lower has room to run.
  • ArgsRoom: the weaker condition that each rule's argument conditions fit, enough for the translated lower_scoped, refusals included.
