A capped rule's charges stay within its limit in every window, and a delegation tree's spending stays within each node's allowance. The first runs in the account; the second is a model result.

The window bound is the evaluator's limit over time: a rule may carry a cap of λ per P ledgers, charged in the same decision that checks the call. The delegation-tree budget is rule-core's lifetime limit for a tree of grants, which the account does not use.

{{statements:T6}}

Ledger 0 and the second form of Theorem 6

{{ref:stmt:cap-window-safety}} has two forms. The first bounds every window \((\max(0, L - P), L]\) for every ledger \(L\) and needs no assumption about ledgers. The second, proved for the model, bounds every \(P\) consecutive ledgers: with no assumption when they start at ledger 1 or later, and from ledger 0 when every authorization is at ledger 1 or later. At ledger 0 the eviction cutoff \(\max(0, 0 - P)\) is 0, so a record keeps nothing from ledger 0 and each spend there is checked alone. rule-core's Cap.period_ledger_zero records two spends of the whole limit at ledger 0, after which the \(P\) ledgers from 0 hold twice the limit, while every window of the first form still holds at most the limit, since none contains ledger 0. Stellar's ledgers start at 1. The cap-window instance in the card above has a toggle for ledger 0.

The bound on chain

The evaluator keeps at most {{fact:evaluator-history-bound}} entries in a record. On chain the binding limit is the size of one ledger entry: a spending record takes {{fact:spend-record-base-bytes}} bytes plus {{fact:spend-record-per-spend-bytes}} per spend, so the network's {{fact:ledger-entry-limit-bytes}}-byte entry limit holds {{fact:spends-per-window}} spends per window. Further capped transfers through that rule fail until older spends leave the window, so a grantee can exhaust its own rule's window with small transfers. This is a refusal, never an excess: the theorem still holds.
