A grant has to hold against the app that holds it and against whoever submits its transactions; the account as a whole also faces a thief of the owner's key, guardians and the registry's operator.

{{ref:tab:tab-adversaries}} lists five adversaries, what each can do, what Perch claims against each with its evidence, and what remains. Proved means a Lean theorem checked by Lean's kernel; tested means tests of the built contracts. The Perch paper treats the first two in full; this section adds the other three and the goals behind all five.

{{table:tab-adversaries}}

Goals

  • G1 Containment. No grant rule allows a call to the account, its adopted recovery controller, or the zero-knowledge adapter and pool of its recovery configuration. Proved for lowering under Doc.Valid, a well-formedness condition on documents ({{ref:stmt:grant-target-containment}}, {{ref:stmt:the-containment-chain}}); the runtime recheck is tested.
  • G2 Permission soundness. An accepted authorization is allowed by the rules it cites. Proved for the evaluator ({{ref:stmt:acceptance-implies-permission}}); signer authentication, rule selection and context conversion are tested.
  • G3 Window safety. A capped rule's charges in any window stay within its limit. Proved for the evaluator from empty records ({{ref:stmt:cap-window-safety}}); rule selection and record persistence are tested; on chain a window holds at most {{fact:spends-per-window}} spends.
  • G4 Recovery invalidation. After a completed recovery, no earlier grant authorizes through the account; allowances it created stay usable. Shown in the account model ({{ref:stmt:recovery-invalidation}}), by shell tests, by the executed reference run and in the testnet record ({{ref:sec:recovery}}).
  • G5 Signature binding. An external signer's signature binds the account, the entry and the selected rule IDs. Tested, with delegated signers excluded; what Perch proves gives a paper proof.
  • G6 Fail-closed refusal. A refusal writes nothing. Tested; in the model a refusal yields no next state.

In Saltzer and Schroeder's terms {{cite:saltzer-schroeder}}, __check_auth mediates every require_auth on the account that the host satisfies from an authorization entry; one satisfied by invoker authority, as in the owner's execute, never reaches it. Not goals: budgets shared across grants; effects outside authorization, such as token allowances, the owner's execute and funds held by other contracts; confidentiality; the correctness of verifiers; and what a wallet displays.

The grantee and the relayer, briefly

The Perch paper sets out what a rogue app key (A1) and a submitter (A2) can still do. In short: a key holds the union of every live rule that names it; caps are per rule and restart when a rule is replaced; allowances, funds held by other contracts and the owner's execute lie outside grants; a token rule with no function list also allows approve; delegated signers do not bind rule IDs; and a refused entry can be resubmitted until it expires (the Perch paper's academic edition, Threat Model).

Owner-key theft and recovery (A3, A4)

Losing every factor the recovery condition needs remains loss, and Protected without an enrolled baseline protects against a stolen owner key no better than Loss, under which a thief can cancel any recovery. A full guardian quorum, or whoever holds the zero-knowledge secret, is trusted: recovery cannot tell two holders of one factor apart. Outside an authorized recovery attempt, the owner may schedule a code upgrade and execute it after {{fact:upgrade-delay-ledgers}} ledgers ({{fact:upgrade-delay-days}}); any change of the recovery generation makes the request stale, and under Protected the recovery condition must approve the exact Wasm hash. New code inherits the account's storage, including the revoked set and the generation, and nothing in the old code can make it honour them; the proofs cover the examined code, not replacement code.

One documented deferral bears on recovery: the account consumes a completion marker by the target's canonical identity, not its exact bytes. If a completion authorization whose apply_doc never ran left a marker, an owner-authorized write of a differently spelled version of the same target in the same ledger is classified as the completion and runs its revocation, nullifier and generation effects (issue {{fact:completion-marker-issue}}). It cannot install a different policy.

Components the account trusts (A5)

The account trusts its verifiers to check evidence, and the recovery controller its account document names to decide completions. Unlike verifiers, the controller gets no registry provenance check, so wallets should pin the published controller's content address. The perch/verifiers registry's manager admits verifier names and its admin can upgrade the registry; one key holds both roles on testnet and mainnet, and names resolved by later installs follow the manager. The WebAuthn verifier does not bind an origin, and a wallet must show what the signed payload commits to.

Resources

Bounded records and arithmetic can refuse a logically permitted action. Documents are capped at {{fact:doc-max-signers}} signers, {{fact:doc-max-rules}} rules, {{fact:doc-max-canonical-bytes}} canonical bytes and {{fact:doc-max-rule-name-bytes}}-byte rule names; the caps are meant to keep a thief holding the owner key from making recovery too expensive to complete. Perch's budget report measured a lost-key completion at those caps (combined, ZK rotation, in process) at {{fact:worst-case-completion-share}} of the {{fact:tx-instruction-limit}} per-transaction instruction limit; disk-read bytes are not yet measured. The account's renew does not extend installed-grant records, so an idle account may need a restore before its next transaction ({{ref:tab:tab-ending}}).
