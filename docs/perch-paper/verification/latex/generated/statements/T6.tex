\begin{perchstatement}{6}{Cap-window safety.}\label{stmt:cap-window-safety}
\[\begin{aligned}&\mathit{spent}_j(R, \varnothing, \vec a) = ((t_k, x_k))_{k=1}^{m}\\
&\quad \implies \forall L.\ \sum_{k:\ \max(0,\,L - P_j) < t_k \le L} x_k \;\le\; \lambda_j\\
&\quad \land\ \bigl((\forall a \in \vec a.\ \ell_a \ge 1) \implies\\
&\qquad \forall s.\ \sum_{k:\ s \le t_k < s + P_j} x_k \;\le\; \lambda_j\bigr)\end{aligned}\]
From empty records, in any run, counting its accepted authorizations, the charges made to a capped rule with a non-negative limit after $L-P$ and through $L$ sum to at most its limit, for every ledger $L$. The model also bounds every $P$ consecutive ledgers that start at ledger~1 or later, and those from ledger~0 when no authorization is at ledger~0.
\statementhypotheses{One fixed rule set. Rule \(j\) of \(R\) has the cap \((\lambda_j, P_j)\) with \(\lambda_j \ge 0\); empty records. The second conjunct is proved for the model only. Without its hypothesis it fails from ledger 0: two charges of the limit at ledger 0 are both recorded (\code{Cap.\allowbreak{}period\_\allowbreak{}ledger\_\allowbreak{}zero}).}
