\paragraph{What oz-policy-builder documents.} Its interpreter sees one authorized call at a
time, so a call that authorizes two contexts needs a rule for each
(\srcpath{docs/architecture.md:307-309}); relating the calls of a transaction is the
execution adapter's job, since a rule bound to the adapter is honoured only for a
call in a batch the adapter checked (\srcpath{docs/stellar.md:191-195}). Totals come
only from outside the interpreter: it ``does not see what a transaction moved,
keep rolling totals or count calls'', and limits over time come from a token
allowance and its expiry or from OpenZeppelin's \code{spending\_limit} beside it
(\srcpath{docs/stellar.md:404-407}), which the installer attaches only to a rule that
pins \code{transfer} on a token (\srcpath{packages/policy-synth/src/install.ts:957-967}).
That policy uses Perch's window boundary (\fact{oz-spending-window}) and keeps at
most \fact{oz-spending-history-max} entries; oz-policy-builder's authors deployed
the OpenZeppelin policies they pin and state that they did not audit them
(\srcpath{evidence/README.md:154-157}). OpenZeppelin publishes audit reports for
its library; the \fact{oz-v080-tag} diff audit lists \code{spending\_limit.rs}
and the smart account's \code{storage.rs} in scope (\code{stellar-contracts}
\srcpath{audits/}, v0.8.0 report, p.~4). Several signers need a threshold policy: a
rule with any policy accepts one authenticated signer unless
\code{simple\_threshold} or \code{weighted\_threshold} is attached, and the
installer requires one unless the owners set \code{anyOne}
(\srcpath{README.md:122-125}). Changing and ending a rule are separate steps. The
interpreter pins a policed rule to its signer set and refuses with error
\fact{opb-error-signers-changed} once the set changes, so changing who signs means
adding a rule and removing the old one, or removing the policy and adding it
again with its admins (\srcpath{docs/stellar.md:552-554}); rules are removed one at a time; and
removing a rule with only rule~0's signatures leaves the interpreter's entry for
it, never read again, until it archives, because the interpreter's
\code{uninstall} needs the policy admins' own authorization
(\srcpath{docs/stellar.md:548-562}).

\paragraph{What it buys.} The interpreter is fixed code, reviewed once, and a
policy is data, so ``a new combination of constraints needs no new contract and
no re-audit'' (\srcpath{README.md:21-24}). Because each policy is a contract, a
predicate can read live state, up to \fact{opb-interpreter-max-reads} values from
other contracts, such as an oracle price or a registry
(\srcpath{dsl.rs:409-423}; \srcpath{docs/stellar.md:369-375}). OpenZeppelin's own policies
attach to the same rule, and one can be added or swapped without changing the
others. In
exchange, each guarantee holds while the contract that gives it is attached: the
window bound while \code{spending\_limit} is on the rule, the threshold while a
threshold policy is. Each policy keeps its own state, and a write by an earlier
policy is rolled back with the call when a later one refuses
(Table~\ref{tab:tab-walkthrough}, step~4).

\paragraph{What Perch does instead, and what that costs.} A Perch grant is one
document decided by one evaluator linked into the account: target, functions,
argument conditions, threshold, expiry, generation and window cap are checked
together, against rules and spending records held only in the account. There is
nothing to attach or order, and one install writes a grant whose rules span
several contracts. The cost is the converse of what composition buys. A rule can
say less: \fact{doc-conditions-count} conjunctive argument conditions
(\fact{doc-conditions}), no disjunction or negation, no bound on one call's
amount, and no reads of other contracts. And because the evaluator is linked into
the account, a new kind of condition needs a new evaluator, new proofs and a new
account build, which an existing account reaches only through its owner's
upgrade, at least \fact{upgrade-delay-ledgers} ledgers after it is scheduled
(Perch \srcpath{perch-recovery-interface/src/account.rs:15}).
