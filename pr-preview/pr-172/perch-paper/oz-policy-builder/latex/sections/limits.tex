\paragraph{What was run.} Both runs of the task (\S\ref{sec:same-task}) are one
task, on soroban-sim's recording-mode host build without exact metering, with
different signer kinds on the two sides; they show verdicts and call structure,
not cost.

\paragraph{Proposed measurement.} We propose, and have not run, five workloads
under one harness with exact metering, one host version and Ed25519 signers on
both sides, reporting CPU instructions, memory, ledger I/O, fees and
cross-contract calls: W1, one capped transfer; W2, a losing guess and the
transfer it makes; W3, one grant install; W4, ending authority over $k$ grants
(one recovery against $k$ calls to \code{remove\_context\_rule}); W5, storage
after $n$ transfers.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} Until W1--W5 run, we claim no difference in
calls, cost, speed or code size. We do not claim that Perch
binds more in general, or that oz-policy-builder is unproven or runs on mainnet
(OpenZeppelin's \fact{oz-v072-tag} policies do). We do not claim that \code{spending\_limit} and Perch's cap behave alike
beyond their shared eviction boundary, or that Perch lacking the features
behind Table~\ref{tab:tab-disclosed}'s oz-policy-builder rows is a security property.
