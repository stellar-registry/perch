The steps above need no one but the app: the app signs, and the account decides. When a person does review a transaction before signing it, such as a user paying at a checkout, a wallet can show them what the transaction asks for and preview the account's verdict. The sign dialog below is a preview of such a dialog, embedded with a toy café app whose grant is separate from the shop grant. Pick an order: the dialog simulates it as it opens, then you approve or reject; the three orders are within the cap, over it, and the tampered checkout.

The dialog shows every authorization the request asks for

The sign dialog decodes every entry from the transaction alone, with no network: it leads with what the user approves in plain words and keeps the whole tree they would authorize under Details.

The whole tree matters because a signed entry is published with its transaction. The café demo's third order comes from a tampered checkout that adds a second entry, a transfer of 4 XLM to another address, which the transaction never uses. If the user signs, anyone can submit that entry later, until it expires; in the recorded run, submitting it alone moved 4 XLM to the other address. The dialog marks the entry as unused, so the user can refuse. A grant that pins the recipient, such as the shop grant, refuses a transfer to any other address, which the dual simulation's "Pay 1 elsewhere" preset shows.

The preview shows the verdict and the budget left

Before the user signs, the dialog previews the request in the simulator. The preview records the transaction independently and flags any entry the request carries that the recording does not need. It then runs the account's own __check_auth with enforcing simulation, which gives Perch's verdict, the change in the account's balance and how much of the grant's budget remains; the recording simulation that finds a transaction's entries skips __check_auth and cannot predict a refusal. In the café demo, a 5 XLM order is accepted, and the budget left falls from 7 to 2 XLM. An 8 XLM order is refused with contract error {{fact:auth-error-rule-eval-refused}}, and the budget stays at 7 XLM.
