\begin{perchstatement}{1}{Total equivalence of the evaluator.}\label{stmt:total-equivalence-of-the-evaluator}
\[\begin{aligned}&\forall x.\ \exists y.\ \mathit{authorize}_{\mathrm{T}}(x) = \mathsf{ok}(y)\\
&\qquad \land\ \llbracket y \rrbracket = \mathit{authorize}(\llbracket x \rrbracket)\end{aligned}\]
Here \(x\) is the seven Rust inputs, and \(\mathsf{ok}\) is Aeneas's success, so neither a panic nor divergence. On every input the translated evaluator succeeds, never panicking or diverging, and its value denotes the model's result on the denoted inputs; this says nothing about allocation failure or compiled Wasm.

\begin{perchstatement}{4}{Attenuation narrows authority.}\label{stmt:attenuation-narrows-authority}
\[\mathit{Derives}(R, R') \implies R' \sqsubseteq R\]
Successful attenuation by the ten attenuation steps yields a rule set narrower than its parent: under any signers, every call the child allows, the parent allows. Repeated derivation preserves this relationship. \emph{allows} reads no cap or expiry, so the steps that add or tighten them narrow only over runs (Theorem~\ref{stmt:trace-inclusion}); parent and child keep separate records, and neither theorem bounds what they spend together. The account has no entry point that installs an attenuated grant at the pinned build.
\statementhypotheses{Each derivation step is one of the ten attenuation steps and succeeds}

\begin{perchstatement}{6}{Cap-window safety.}\label{stmt:cap-window-safety}
\[\begin{aligned}&\mathit{spent}_j(R, \varnothing, \vec a) = ((t_k, x_k))_{k=1}^{m}\\
&\quad \implies \forall L.\ \sum_{k:\ \max(0,\,L - P_j) < t_k \le L} x_k \;\le\; \lambda_j\\
&\quad \land\ \bigl((\forall a \in \vec a.\ \ell_a \ge 1) \implies\\
&\qquad \forall s.\ \sum_{k:\ s \le t_k < s + P_j} x_k \;\le\; \lambda_j\bigr)\end{aligned}\]
From empty records, in any run, counting its accepted authorizations, the charges made to a capped rule with a non-negative limit after $L-P$ and through $L$ sum to at most its limit, for every ledger $L$. The model also bounds every $P$ consecutive ledgers that start at ledger~1 or later, and those from ledger~0 when no authorization is at ledger~0.
\statementhypotheses{Rule \(j\) of \(R\) has the cap \((\lambda_j, P_j)\) with \(\lambda_j \ge 0\); empty records. The second conjunct is proved for the model only. Without its hypothesis it fails from ledger 0: two charges of the limit at ledger 0 are both recorded (\code{Cap.\allowbreak{}period\_\allowbreak{}ledger\_\allowbreak{}zero}).}

\begin{perchstatement}{8}{Grant target containment.}\label{stmt:grant-target-containment}
\[\begin{aligned}&\mathit{lowerScoped}(d, A, X, g) = R\\
&\quad \land\ \mathit{Derives}(R, R')\\
&\quad \land\ \mathit{allows}(R', \mathsf{call}(C, f, \vec v), S)\\
&\qquad \implies C \neq A \;\land\; C \notin X\end{aligned}\]
\(\mathit{lowerScoped}(d, A, X, g)\) is the rule set the account installs for grant document \(d\) at address \(A\), with excluded set \(X\) and generation \(g\); it allows no call to \(A\) or to an element of \(X\), and attenuation preserves this. In the account, \(X\) is its adopted recovery controller and the zero-knowledge adapter and pool of its recovery configuration.
